# Codebook

*Work in progress. The coding structure is approved; the methodology summary describes the intended completed workflow and will be checked before submission.*

The Language page of this mini-site compares the language used by B.C.'s five independent regional destination management organizations (RDMOs) to describe their work and the people they serve. The aim was to see whether their priorities shifted from economic and industry development toward community development, and whether their language placed more emphasis on residents over time.

About pages from 1995–2026 were selected based on their availability through the organizations' websites and archived copies from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine. If a collected page had no significant changes from the previous available version, only the earlier version was included (e.g., if the wording in 2004 and 2005 was identical, 2004 was kept and 2005 was excluded from the timeline).

The pages were divided into clauses and coded using the structure below. Systemic functional linguistics (SFL) helped identify what was happening and who was involved. The coding also identified the priorities and beneficiaries expressed in the text. Critical discourse analysis (CDA) helped examine these changes in their wider context.

Earl independently coded the earliest and latest available About pages for each RDMO. Codex used this codebook and those pages as examples to propose codes for the remaining pages. Earl reviewed the proposed codes and either accepted or edited them. The charts and tables show frequency counts, with footnotes identifying the percentage of codes Earl originally assigned, accepted or edited, and any Codex proposals still awaiting review.

The examples below illustrate the categories in a tourism context; they are not quotations or coded findings from the corpus.

## Coding fields

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `process_type` | Process type | What kind of action, experience or state is represented? Example: “We promote the region” uses a material process. |
| `process` | Process | Which words express the process? Example: “We promote the region” → promote. |
| `participants` | Participants | Who or what is involved, and in what role? Example: “We support operators” → We (Actor); operators (Goal). |
| `voice` | Voice | Is the process expressed in active or passive voice? Example: “The region is promoted by the RDMO” is passive. |
| `institutional_priority` | Institutional priority | What objective or area of work is expressed? Example: “We attract visitors” → destination marketing. |
| `represented_beneficiary` | Represented beneficiary | Who is presented as receiving a benefit or support? Example: “We provide grants to members” → organizational members. |

## Process types

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `material` | Material | Doing or happening, such as building or developing. Example: “We develop marketing campaigns.” |
| `mental` | Mental | Thinking, feeling, wanting or perceiving. Example: “We believe tourism can benefit communities.” |
| `relational` | Relational | Being, having, describing or identifying. Example: “We are a regional tourism association.” |
| `verbal` | Verbal | Saying or communicating. Example: “We announce new tourism initiatives.” |
| `behavioural` | Behavioural | Observable behaviour, such as laughing or breathing. Example: “Visitors watch the annual festival.” |
| `existential` | Existential | Expressing the existence of something. Example: “There are five visitor centres in the region.” |
| `unclear` | Unclear | The process type needs further review. Example: “We see opportunities” could refer to perception or recognition; review the context. |

## Participant roles

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `actor` | Actor | Material: who or what performs the action? Example: “The association promotes the region” → The association. |
| `goal` | Goal | Material: who or what is acted upon or affected? Example: “We develop new trails” → new trails. |
| `recipient` | Recipient | Material: who receives something? Example: “We give members grants” → members. |
| `client` | Client | Material: for whom is the action performed? Example: “We prepare marketing materials for operators” → operators. |
| `scope` | Scope | Material: what specifies the action without being affected by it? Example: “We conduct a tourism survey” → a tourism survey. |
| `senser` | Senser | Mental: who thinks, feels, wants or perceives? Example: “We believe in sustainable tourism” → We. |
| `phenomenon` | Phenomenon | Mental: what is thought, felt, wanted or perceived? Example: “We value community partnerships” → community partnerships. |
| `carrier` | Carrier | Relational: who or what is being described? Example: “Our region is diverse” → Our region. |
| `attribute` | Attribute | Relational: what quality or status is assigned? Example: “Our region is diverse” → diverse. |
| `token` | Token | Relational: who or what is being identified? Example: “Our mission is tourism promotion” → Our mission. |
| `value` | Value | Relational: what supplies the identifying meaning? Example: “Our mission is tourism promotion” → tourism promotion. |
| `possessor` | Possessor | Relational: who or what has something? Example: “Our association has 200 members” → Our association. |
| `possessed` | Possessed | Relational: what is possessed? Example: “Our association has 200 members” → 200 members. |
| `sayer` | Sayer | Verbal: who communicates? Example: “The association announces new programs” → The association. |
| `receiver` | Receiver | Verbal: who receives the communication? Example: “We inform members about changes” → members. |
| `verbiage` | Verbiage | Verbal: what is said or communicated? Example: “We announce our annual priorities” → our annual priorities. |
| `target` | Target | Verbal: who or what is addressed by a directed verbal action, such as praise or criticism? Example: “We thank local tourism operators” → local tourism operators. |
| `behaver` | Behaver | Behavioural: who performs the behaviour? Example: “Visitors watch the parade” → Visitors. |
| `behaviour` | Behaviour | Behavioural: what behaviour is expressed by an additional participant? Example: “Visitors breathe a sigh of relief” → a sigh of relief. |
| `existent` | Existent | Existential: who or what exists? Example: “There are tourism businesses throughout the region” → tourism businesses. |
| `other_role` | Other | A participant with a role outside this list. Example: “We show visitors the region” → review any participant outside the listed roles. |
| `unclear_role` | Unclear | The participant's role needs further review. Example: “We support development” → review whether development is Goal or Scope. |

Use the original words for processes and participants. A participant can be explicit, implicit or omitted. An implicit participant is recoverable from the text; an omitted participant cannot be identified from it.

## Voice

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `active` | Active | The clause uses active voice, as in “We develop programs.” Example: “We market the destination.” |
| `passive` | Passive | The clause uses passive voice, as in “Programs are developed.” Example: “The destination is marketed by our association.” |
| `not_applicable` | Not applicable | The active/passive distinction does not apply to this clause. Example: “We are a non-profit association.” |
| `unclear` | Unclear | Voice needs further review. Example: “Our work is supported” may require context to identify a process and its voice. |

## Institutional priorities

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `destination_marketing` | Destination marketing | Promoting destinations and attracting or retaining visitors. Example: “We attract visitors to the region.” |
| `economic_development` | Economic development | Growing spending, employment, revenue, investment or economic activity. Example: “We increase regional tourism revenue.” |
| `industry_development` | Industry development | Supporting tourism businesses, operators or industry capacity. Example: “We train tourism operators.” |
| `community_development` | Community development | Improving residents' well-being, quality of life, services or community capacity. Example: “We improve residents’ quality of life.” |
| `environmental_stewardship` | Environmental stewardship | Protecting, restoring or responsibly managing the environment. Example: “We protect natural areas.” |
| `indigenous_tourism` | Indigenous tourism | Supporting Indigenous-led tourism, enterprises or experiences. Example: “We support Indigenous-led visitor experiences.” |
| `coordination_partnerships` | Coordination and partnerships | Collaborating, consulting or coordinating work with others. Example: “We work with local tourism associations.” |
| `other` | Other | An expressed priority outside these categories. Example: “We preserve our organization’s archives.” |
| `not_expressed` | Not expressed | No priority is identified in the text. Example: “Our office is in Kamloops.” |
| `unclear` | Unclear | The priority needs further review. Example: “We support growth” does not specify what grows. |

## Beneficiaries and social groups

These groups identify beneficiaries and material Actors. Mentioning a group does not make it a beneficiary. Benefits must be expressed or clear from the surrounding text. Use the most specific applicable group for each participant.

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `tourism_industry` | Tourism businesses and operators | Tourism enterprises, operators or industry participants. Example: “We train tourism operators.” |
| `members` | Organizational members | People or organizations identified through membership. Example: “Our members receive marketing support.” |
| `residents_communities` | Residents and communities | Local people or communities, rather than a geographical area. Example: “Our programs benefit local residents.” |
| `visitors_travellers` | Visitors and travellers | Tourists, visitors or travellers. Example: “We improve the visitor experience.” |
| `indigenous_peoples_nations` | Indigenous peoples and Nations | Indigenous peoples, communities or Nations. Example: “We provide support to First Nations communities.” |
| `government` | Government | Governments or public-sector authorities. Example: “We provide municipalities with tourism data.” |
| `rdmo` | RDMO itself | The regional destination organization. Example: “The program strengthens our association.” |
| `other` | Other | A group or entity outside these categories. Example: “Our campaign supports local charities.” |
| `not_expressed` | Not expressed | No beneficiary is identifiable in the text. Example: “We develop a regional plan” names no beneficiary. |
| `non_social` | Non-social Actor | An object, event or abstract concept acting in a material process; used for agency only. Example: “Tourism revenue supports jobs” → Tourism revenue is a non-social Actor. |
| `unclear` | Unclear | The group's identity or role needs further review. Example: “We support stakeholders” leaves the group unspecified. |

## CDA

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `text` | Text | What do the words and grammatical choices do? Example: Examine who is the Actor in “We develop tourism.” |
| `discursive_practice` | Discursive practice | Who produced the text, for whom and how is it circulated? Example: Ask whether an About page addresses members or visitors. |
| `social_practice` | Social practice | Whose interests are foregrounded and what is presented as normal? Example: Examine how an About page frames tourism growth as a shared community goal. |

These dimensions guide interpretation of selected passages rather than adding compulsory clause codes.

## Coding provenance

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `human_original` | Human original | Independently coded by the researcher. Example: Earl selected and coded an About-page clause without an AI proposal. |
| `human_accepted` | Human accepted | A machine proposal reviewed and accepted by the researcher. Example: Earl reviewed a proposed process type and kept it. |
| `human_edited` | Human edited | A machine proposal reviewed and revised by the researcher. Example: Earl changed a proposed beneficiary after review. |
| `machine_proposed` | Machine proposed | A machine proposal awaiting human review. Example: Codex proposed a process type; Earl has not reviewed it. |
