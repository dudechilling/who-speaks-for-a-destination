# Codebook

*Work in progress. The coding structure is approved; the methodology summary describes the intended completed workflow and will be checked before submission.*

The Language page of this mini-site compares the language used by B.C.'s five independent regional destination management organizations (RDMOs) to describe their work and the people they serve. The aim was to see whether their priorities shifted from economic and industry development toward community development, and whether their language placed more emphasis on residents over time.

About pages from 1995–2026 were selected based on their availability through the organizations' websites and archived copies from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine. If a collected page had no significant changes from the previous available version, only the earlier version was included (e.g., if the wording in 2004 and 2005 was identical, 2004 was kept and 2005 was excluded from the timeline).

The pages were divided into clauses and coded using the structure below. Systemic functional linguistics (SFL) helped identify what was happening and who was involved. The coding also identified the priorities and beneficiaries expressed in the text. Critical discourse analysis (CDA) helped examine these changes in their wider context.

Earl independently coded the earliest and latest available About pages for each RDMO. Codex used this codebook and those pages as examples to propose codes for the remaining pages. Earl reviewed the proposed codes and either accepted or edited them. The charts and tables show frequency counts, with footnotes identifying the percentage of codes Earl originally assigned, accepted or edited, and any Codex proposals still awaiting review.

## Coding fields

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `process_type` | Process type | What kind of action, experience or state is represented? |
| `process` | Process | Which words express the process? |
| `participants` | Participants | Who or what is involved, and in what role? |
| `voice` | Voice | Is the process expressed in active or passive voice? |
| `institutional_priority` | Institutional priority | What objective or area of work is expressed? |
| `represented_beneficiary` | Represented beneficiary | Who is presented as receiving a benefit or support? |

## Process types

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `material` | Material | Doing or happening, such as building or developing. |
| `mental` | Mental | Thinking, feeling, wanting or perceiving. |
| `relational` | Relational | Being, having, describing or identifying. |
| `verbal` | Verbal | Saying or communicating. |
| `behavioural` | Behavioural | Observable behaviour, such as laughing or breathing. |
| `existential` | Existential | Expressing the existence of something. |
| `unclear` | Unclear | The process type needs further review. |

## Participant roles

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `actor` | Actor | Material: who or what performs the action? |
| `goal` | Goal | Material: who or what is acted upon or affected? |
| `recipient` | Recipient | Material: who receives something? |
| `client` | Client | Material: for whom is the action performed? |
| `scope` | Scope | Material: what specifies the action without being affected by it? |
| `senser` | Senser | Mental: who thinks, feels, wants or perceives? |
| `phenomenon` | Phenomenon | Mental: what is thought, felt, wanted or perceived? |
| `carrier` | Carrier | Relational: who or what is being described? |
| `attribute` | Attribute | Relational: what quality or status is assigned? |
| `token` | Token | Relational: who or what is being identified? |
| `value` | Value | Relational: what supplies the identifying meaning? |
| `possessor` | Possessor | Relational: who or what has something? |
| `possessed` | Possessed | Relational: what is possessed? |
| `sayer` | Sayer | Verbal: who communicates? |
| `receiver` | Receiver | Verbal: who receives the communication? |
| `verbiage` | Verbiage | Verbal: what is said or communicated? |
| `target` | Target | Verbal: who or what is addressed by a directed verbal action, such as praise or criticism? |
| `behaver` | Behaver | Behavioural: who performs the behaviour? |
| `behaviour` | Behaviour | Behavioural: what behaviour is expressed by an additional participant? |
| `existent` | Existent | Existential: who or what exists? |
| `other_role` | Other | A participant with a role outside this list. |
| `unclear_role` | Unclear | The participant's role needs further review. |

Use the original words for processes and participants. A participant can be explicit, implicit or omitted. An implicit participant is recoverable from the text; an omitted participant cannot be identified from it.

## Voice

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `active` | Active | The clause uses active voice, as in “We develop programs.” |
| `passive` | Passive | The clause uses passive voice, as in “Programs are developed.” |
| `not_applicable` | Not applicable | The active/passive distinction does not apply to this clause. |
| `unclear` | Unclear | Voice needs further review. |

## Institutional priorities

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `destination_marketing` | Destination marketing | Promoting destinations and attracting or retaining visitors. |
| `economic_development` | Economic development | Growing spending, employment, revenue, investment or economic activity. |
| `industry_development` | Industry development | Supporting tourism businesses, operators or industry capacity. |
| `community_development` | Community development | Improving residents' well-being, quality of life, services or community capacity. |

| `unclear` | Unclear | The priority needs further review. |

## Beneficiaries and social groups

These groups identify beneficiaries and material Actors. Mentioning a group does not make it a beneficiary. Benefits must be expressed or clear from the surrounding text. Use the most specific applicable group for each participant.

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `tourism_industry` | Tourism businesses and operators | Tourism enterprises, operators or industry participants. |
| `members` | Organizational members | People or organizations identified through membership. |
| `residents_communities` | Residents and communities | Local people or communities, rather than a geographical area. |
| `visitors_travellers` | Visitors and travellers | Tourists, visitors or travellers. |
| `indigenous_peoples_nations` | Indigenous peoples and Nations | Indigenous peoples, communities or Nations. |
| `government` | Government | Governments or public-sector authorities. |
| `rdmo` | RDMO itself | The regional destination organization. |
| `other` | Other | A group or entity outside these categories. |
| `not_expressed` | Not expressed | No beneficiary is identifiable in the text. |
| `non_social` | Non-social Actor | An object, event or abstract concept acting in a material process; used for agency only. |
| `unclear` | Unclear | The group's identity or role needs further review. |

## CDA

| ID | Label | Tooltip |
|---|---|---|
| `text` | Text | What do the words and grammatical choices do? |
| `discursive_practice` | Discursive practice | Who produced the text, for whom and how is it circulated? |
| `social_practice` | Social practice | Whose interests are foregrounded and what is presented as normal? |

These dimensions guide interpretation of selected passages rather than adding compulsory clause codes.
