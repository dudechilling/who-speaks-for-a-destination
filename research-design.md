# CMNS 3500 — Project 3
# Historical Analysis of Destination Governance in British Columbia

Status: Provisional research design
Approach: Diachronic corpus analysis, Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL), and supporting Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA).

## 1. Research Purpose

This project examines how British Columbia's regional tourism organizations have changed the way they describe their purposes, priorities, responsibilities, and relationships with the destinations they represent.

The central interest is the historical movement between different ways of framing tourism, including:

- Economic growth and tourism revenue
- Destination marketing and visitor attraction
- Community development and resident welfare
- Environmental protection and land stewardship
- Indigenous participation and governance
- Destination management and sustainable development

We want to examine whether certain frames become more or less prominent over time, how organizations construct authority through language, and whether changes in discourse correspond with changes in organizational governance and definitions of success.

The study will not assume that newer language represents genuine institutional change, nor that economic growth and environmental stewardship are necessarily incompatible.

Our objective is to establish what the historical evidence supports.

## 2. Research Questions

Primary research question:

How have the dominant frames used by British Columbia's regional tourism organizations changed over time, and how do these changes correspond with their governance structures and stated measures of success?

Supporting questions:

1. How do SFL features reveal changes in the representation of tourism growth, community development, environmental stewardship, and institutional authority?

2. How has the professional and institutional composition of regional tourism boards changed over the same period?

3. How have the outcomes these organizations prioritize and report measuring changed, and where do those changes align with or diverge from their public language?

## 3. Historical and Organizational Scope

We are interested in approximately 30–40 years of institutional history, tentatively 1985–2025.

The starting year, final period, and sampling frequency remain subject to archival availability and the course assignment requirements.

Initial organizations:

1. Tourism Vancouver Island / 4VI Group
2. Thompson Okanagan Tourism Association (TOTA)
3. Kootenay Rockies Tourism (KRT)
4. Northern BC Tourism Association (NBCTA)
5. Cariboo Chilcotin Coast Tourism Association (CCCTA)

Historical organizational names, predecessor institutions, changes in responsibilities, and structural reorganizations must be documented.

The original proposal considered approximately 200 documents, potentially one annual document per organization over 40 years.

This remains a collection ambition, not an approved corpus size.

The research must not assume that these organizations have comparable annual archives extending back to the 1980s.

## 4. Research Architecture

We propose three separate but linked datasets.

DATASET A: Historical SFL Corpus
DATASET B: Historical Board Composition
DATASET C: Organizational Priorities and Performance Measures

Each dataset should be linked through organization, year, and source identifiers.

Keeping them separate allows us to compare different forms of evidence without confusing linguistic representations with actual governance arrangements or organizational performance.

## 5. Dataset A — Diachronic SFL Corpus

Purpose:

Identify historical changes in how regional tourism organizations use language to communicate their roles, priorities, responsibilities, and relationships with other actors.

Preferred primary sources:

- Annual reports
- Annual statements from board chairs or executives
- Organizational mission and vision statements
- Strategic priorities within annual reports

Strategic plans and other documents may provide supplementary evidence, but document genre must be considered when making comparisons.

Proposed analytical dimensions:

A. Dominant Discursive Frames

Identify which institutional priorities are emphasized.

Candidate categories include:

- Tourism growth and economic development
- Marketing and destination promotion
- Community development and resident welfare
- Environmental stewardship and conservation
- Indigenous governance and participation
- Visitor experience
- Destination management

These are preliminary interpretive categories, not established SFL grammatical categories.

Allow primary and secondary frames where a passage expresses multiple priorities.

The final coding rules must define how frame dominance is assessed.

B. Ideational Metafunction — Transitivity

Examine how organizations represent actions, events, relationships, and participants.

Relevant features include:

- Material processes
- Mental processes
- Relational processes
- Verbal processes
- Behavioural processes
- Existential processes
- Participant roles associated with each process

Questions include:

Who is represented as acting?

Who makes decisions?

Who receives benefits?

Who is affected by decisions?

Are residents, Indigenous governments, businesses, and environmental interests represented as actors, participants, beneficiaries, or objects of management?

Process types must be classified according to SFL definitions, not simple keyword lists.

C. Interpersonal Metafunction — Modality

Examine how organizations express:

- Obligations
- Commitments
- Possibilities
- Predictions
- Permission
- Certainty and uncertainty

Compare how organizations express commitments across economic, community, and environmental priorities.

Do not assume a modal verb has the same function in every context.

D. Textual Metafunction — Theme/Rheme

Examine how organizations structure clauses and foreground information.

Questions include:

Which topics are placed in prominent thematic positions?

How are statements about stewardship, development, growth, and community participation developed?

Do recurring thematic patterns change historically?

Theme and Rheme must be identified using proper SFL grammatical criteria, not simply by separating introductory phrases from the remainder of sentences.

E. Representation of Participants

Track representations of:

- Tourism organizations
- Tourism businesses and operators
- Tourists and visitors
- Local residents
- Municipal and provincial governments
- Indigenous governments and organizations
- Environmental interests and ecosystems

Distinguish grammatical roles from actual institutional authority.

A community appearing as an Actor in a clause does not independently establish that it possesses decision-making power.

## 6. Dataset B — Historical Board Composition

Purpose:

Examine the historical composition of regional tourism boards and their institutional relationships.

For each available organization-year observation, record:

- Organization
- Year
- Director's recorded name
- Board position
- Professional affiliation at the time
- Employment or institutional sector
- Appointment or election basis, where documented
- Source document and page
- Uncertainty or missing information

Potential affiliation categories include:

- Hotels and accommodation
- Tourism operators and attractions
- Destination marketing organizations
- Transportation
- Other private businesses
- Local government
- Provincial government
- Indigenous governments or organizations
- Community organizations and nonprofits
- Environmental or conservation organizations
- Other or unknown

Categories may require refinement and may overlap.

Do not infer a director's identity, political views, or constituency from their name, appearance, or employer.

Distinguish professional affiliation from formal representation.

For example, employment by an Indigenous-owned tourism business does not necessarily mean someone was appointed to represent an Indigenous government.

Where records permit, also examine:

- Who could nominate directors
- Who elected or appointed directors
- Voting rights
- Changes to board structure
- Changes to membership eligibility
- Formal representation requirements

The key question is not simply who sat on boards, but whether the distribution of formal authority changed over time.

## 7. Dataset C — Organizational Priorities and Performance

Purpose:

Examine what tourism organizations publicly identified as successful performance and how those priorities changed.

Candidate indicators:

Economic:
- Visitor numbers
- Tourism revenue
- Accommodation occupancy
- Visitor spending
- Business development

Community:
- Resident sentiment
- Employment
- Community economic benefits
- Local participation

Environmental:
- Emissions
- Conservation programs
- Environmental certifications
- Ecological indicators
- Stewardship activities

Governance:
- Stakeholder participation
- Indigenous governance arrangements
- Community consultation
- Formal partnerships
- Changes to decision-making authority

For every indicator, distinguish:

1. An aspiration or stated priority
2. A proposed performance measure
3. An established reporting measure
4. A reported activity
5. A measured result
6. An independently verified outcome

Do not treat these as interchangeable evidence.

This dataset should help determine whether shifts in public language correspond with changes in what organizations actually choose to measure or report.

## 8. Longitudinal Comparison

The intended analysis is historical and comparative.

For each organization, we want to map changes in:

A. Dominant discourse frames
B. SFL linguistic features
C. Board composition and governance
D. Stated priorities and performance measures

The eventual analysis may examine:

- Whether economic-growth framing declines, persists, or changes form
- Whether community and environmental framing becomes more prominent
- Whether changes in governance coincide with changes in language
- Whether organizations change their stated priorities without changing their reporting measures
- Whether some organizations adopt new language earlier than others
- Whether institutional reorganizations correspond with detectable changes in discourse

These are questions to investigate, not predicted findings.

Changes in language could reflect organizational decisions, provincial policy, changing reporting requirements, audience expectations, document genre, or wider social developments.

The analysis must consider these competing explanations.

Associations between board composition and discourse must not be presented as proof that particular directors caused particular linguistic changes.

## 9. Critical Discourse Analysis

CDA will support the interpretation of selected findings.

It is not intended to replace systematic SFL coding.

We may use CDA to examine:

- How institutional authority is constructed and justified
- How organizations represent different stakeholders
- Whose interests appear central or peripheral
- Whether community participation is described as consultation or decision-making
- How economic and environmental priorities are reconciled
- Whether public representations correspond with documented governance arrangements

Avoid treating apparent contradictions as automatic evidence of dishonesty, greenwashing, or stewardship washing.

Such conclusions require additional supporting evidence.

## 10. Intended Outputs

Potential analytical outputs include:

1. A historical timeline of dominant discourse frames by organization.

2. Comparisons of SFL patterns across selected periods.

3. A historical record of board composition and institutional affiliations.

4. Comparisons of the performance indicators organizations emphasized.

5. Integrated visualizations aligning discourse, board composition, and organizational priorities over time.

6. A short qualitative discussion of significant historical changes, competing explanations, and limitations.

Figures and quantitative summaries must be generated from verified observations, not illustrative or simulated values.

## 13. Research Principle

The purpose is not to prove that British Columbia's tourism sector moved from economic growth to environmental stewardship.

The purpose is to investigate how organizations represented their purposes and responsibilities over time, who participated in their governance, and what they identified as successful performance.

The evidence must be allowed to show continuity, change, contradiction, or differences between organizations.

Our methodology must make those outcomes distinguishable rather than predetermining the findings.
